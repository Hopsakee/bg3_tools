Lesson 1

Overview: D&D rules, video-game body

Baldur's Gate 3 runs on a modified version of the Dungeons & Dragons 5th Edition (5e) ruleset. Almost every number you see in the game (a hit chance, a damage range, a "DC 15" in dialogue) comes from those rules.

In tabletop D&D, a human Dungeon Master reads the rules and rolls dice. In BG3 the computer does both. That's good news: you never have to do the maths yourself. But knowing what the game is calculating lets you understand why you missed, why a spell failed, or why a character "feels weak."

The whole system in one sentence

When the outcome of something is uncertain, the game rolls a 20-sided die (d20), adds some bonuses, and compares the total to a target number. Meet or beat the target: success.

Nearly everything else in this course is detail about which bonuses get added and what the target number is.

What does "modified" mean here?

Larian kept the core of 5e (ability scores, proficiency, d20 rolls, spell slots, classes) but changed parts that work badly on a screen or that they wanted to be more tactical: height matters more, the environment reacts to elements, several actions got cheaper, and resting is tied to a camp. Lesson 8 covers these changes one by one.

Lesson 2

Ability scores

Every character has six ability scores, numbers usually between 8 and 20 that describe raw talent. You pick them at character creation and raise them at certain levels.

Scores become modifiers

The score itself is rarely used directly. What matters is the modifier it produces, the number actually added to rolls:

modifier = (score − 10) ÷ 2, rounded down
Score8–910–1112–1314–1516–1718–1920
Modifier−1+0+1+2+3+4+5

Modifier: +2

Practical takeaway: odd scores give the same modifier as the even number below them. Going from 16 to 17 changes nothing on its own; going from 17 to 18 gives +1. BG3 caps scores at 20 unless an item or potion says otherwise.

What each ability does in BG3

STR Strength

Melee weapon hit and damage (non-finesse weapons), how far you Jump, how hard you Shove, how much you can carry before being slowed. Skill: Athletics.

DEX Dexterity

Ranged and finesse weapon hit and damage, initiative, and Armour Class in light or medium armour. Skills: Acrobatics, Sleight of Hand (lockpicking, pickpocketing, disarming traps), Stealth.

CON Constitution

Extra hit points every level, and saving throws to keep concentration on a spell when hit. No skills. Useful for everyone.

INT Intelligence

Spellcasting power for Wizards (and Eldritch Knight / Arcane Trickster subclasses). Skills: Arcana, History, Investigation, Nature, Religion.

WIS Wisdom

Spellcasting power for Clerics, Druids and Rangers; Monk AC and ki abilities. Skills: Perception (spotting traps and hidden things), Insight, Medicine, Survival, Animal Handling.

CHA Charisma

Spellcasting power for Bards, Paladins, Sorcerers and Warlocks. Skills: Persuasion, Deception, Intimidation, Performance. These are the main dialogue skills.

Example: two fighters swing a greatsword

Karlach has STR 17 (+3). Gale has STR 8 (−1). With the same sword, Karlach adds +3 to her attack roll and to her damage; Gale subtracts 1 from both. Over a fight, that 4-point gap means Karlach hits roughly 20% more often and each hit does 4 more damage.

How scores are set at character creation

BG3 uses a point-buy system: every score starts at 8 and you spend 27 points to raise them, with a maximum of 15 before bonuses. You then add a +2 and a +1 to any two abilities you choose. A common starting peak is therefore 17 in your main ability. Levels 4, 8 and 12 give a feat, and one option is "Ability Improvement": +2 to spend across your scores.

Lesson 3

Proficiency

If ability scores are talent, proficiency is training. When your character is proficient in something, they add their proficiency bonus to the roll.

How big is the bonus?

It depends only on your total character level, not on class or ability scores. BG3's level cap is 12, so there are three steps:

Level1–45–89–12
Proficiency bonus+2+3+4

What it applies to

  • Attack rolls with weapons you are proficient with (your class decides which: a Wizard isn't proficient with greatswords, a Fighter is proficient with everything).
  • Spell attack rolls and your spell save DC. Casters are always proficient with their own spells.
  • Skills you picked from your class and background, such as Stealth or Persuasion.
  • Saving throws: each class is proficient in two of the six (a Wizard in INT and WIS, a Fighter in STR and CON).
Example: Astarion picks a lock

Astarion has DEX 17 (+3) and is proficient in Sleight of Hand. At level 3 his bonus is +3 (DEX) +2 (proficiency) = +5. At level 9 the same check is +3 +4 = +7, even if his DEX hasn't changed. Shadowheart, with DEX 13 (+1) and no proficiency, rolls with just +1.

Expertise: double proficiency

Rogues and Bards (and a few other features) can pick skills for Expertise, which doubles the proficiency bonus for those skills. A level 9 Rogue with Expertise in Sleight of Hand adds +8 from proficiency alone.

Wearing armour you aren't proficient with

You can put it on, but you get disadvantage on attacks and on STR/DEX checks and saves, and you can't cast spells. The game warns you in the equipment screen.

Lesson 4

The d20 system

Every uncertain action follows the same recipe: roll a d20, add modifiers, compare to a target. The target is called a Difficulty Class (DC) for checks and saves, or Armour Class (AC) for attacks.

d20 + ability modifier + proficiency (if proficient) + other bonuses ≥ DC → success

An ability check tests whether you can do something: persuade a guard, force open a door, recall lore. In BG3 you see these most in dialogue, where a big die appears on screen with the DC shown before you roll.

Example: a guard wants convincing, Persuasion DC 15. Your Bard has CHA +3 and is proficient (+2): total +5. You need to roll a 10 or higher on the die: 55% chance.

Before you roll, you can often add help: the Guidance cantrip adds 1d4, and spending Inspiration lets you re-roll a failed check.

Advantage: roll two d20s and keep the higher. Disadvantage: roll two, keep the lower. They're applied by circumstance: attacking a sleeping target, attacking while blinded, a spell that grants it, and so on.

  • They don't stack: three sources of advantage still means just two dice.
  • If you have both advantage and disadvantage, they cancel and you roll one die.
  • Advantage is worth roughly +3 to +5 on a typical roll.

Try it: roll a d20

Press a button to roll.

A passive check is a check nobody actively asks for. In tabletop it's a flat number: 10 + your modifier. It represents "taking an average roll."

In BG3 the biggest one is Perception: as you walk around, the game quietly checks each party member's Perception against hidden traps, secret levers and buried chests. When one succeeds, a small icon pops up over that character's head. Nobody in the party has good WIS? You'll walk past a lot of loot, and into a lot of traps.

Passive values are also used as the target for some contested actions, such as Shove (your Athletics against the target's passive Athletics or Acrobatics).

Natural 20s and natural 1s

A natural 20 means the die itself shows 20, before any bonuses. In BG3, a natural 20 on an ability check or an attack roll is always a success, and a natural 1 always fails, no matter the modifiers. (In the tabletop rules this only applies to attacks and, at many tables, death saves. See Lesson 8.)

Lesson 5

Combat fundamentals

Combat in BG3 is turn-based. Everyone rolls for position in the turn order, then each creature takes a turn with a small budget of things it may do.

Initiative and turn order

When a fight starts, every participant rolls initiative and the results are sorted into the portrait bar at the top of the screen. Highest goes first. In tabletop 5e that roll is d20 + DEX modifier; BG3 rolls only a d4 + DEX modifier, so DEX matters far more than luck. A creature that is Surprised (caught unaware) skips its first turn.

BG3 twist: party members who end up with the same initiative share a combined turn. You can switch between them freely and use their actions and movement in any order.

In the hotbar these show as coloured shapes: a green circle for the action, an orange triangle for the bonus action, a yellow bar for movement. Abilities are tagged with the shape they cost.

Example turn
  1. Shadowheart moves 4 m to get line of sight to the wounded Lae'zel (movement: 5 m left).
  2. She casts Healing Word on Lae'zel (bonus action).
  3. She casts Guiding Bolt at the goblin archer (action).
  4. She uses her remaining 5 m to duck behind a pillar.

Her reaction stays available for the enemy turns.

How BG3's action economy differs from tabletop
  • Drinking a potion is a bonus action (tabletop: an action). You can also throw a potion to heal an ally or smash it on a group.
  • Shove and Jump are bonus actions (tabletop: Shove replaces one of your attacks; jumping is part of movement).
  • Throw: you can pick up and throw almost anything, including crates, barrels and small enemies, as an action.
  • Dip: a bonus action to coat your weapon in a nearby fire or poison surface for bonus damage.
  • Weapon actions: special moves like Topple, Cleave or Lacerate come with weapons you're proficient in and recharge on a short rest.
  • Reactions can be set to trigger automatically, prompt you, or stay off, in the Reactions panel.
Hit points, going down, and getting back up

At 0 hit points a party member is Downed. On each of their turns they make a death saving throw (d20, 10+ succeeds). Three successes: stable. Three failures: dead. Taking damage while downed counts as a failure. Any ally can use the Help action on them to bring them back at 1 hit point, and any healing spell or potion does the same with more HP. A dead companion can be revived with a scroll of Revivify or by paying Withers in camp.

Lesson 6

Attacks, Armour Class & saving throws

There are two ways the game decides whether harmful things land: you roll to hit (attack roll vs AC), or the target rolls to avoid it (saving throw vs DC).

Attack rolls

d20 + ability modifier + proficiency ≥ target's AC → hit

The ability is STR for most melee weapons, DEX for ranged weapons, and your choice of either for finesse weapons like daggers and rapiers. On a hit, you roll damage: the weapon's dice plus the same ability modifier.

Armour Class (AC)

AC is how hard you are to hit. It depends on what you wear:

ArmourAC formulaTypical user
None10 + DEXWizard, Sorcerer (often boosted by Mage Armour)
Lightarmour value + DEXRogue, Bard, Warlock
Mediumarmour value + DEX (max +2)Cleric, Druid, Ranger
Heavyarmour value onlyFighter, Paladin
Shield+2 on topanyone proficient
Example: Lae'zel attacks a goblin

Lae'zel (level 4) swings a greatsword. STR 17 gives +3, proficiency +2: total +5. The goblin has AC 15 (leather armour and a shield). She needs a 10 or more on the d20. That's the "55%" the game shows when you hover the target.

She rolls 13: 13 + 5 = 18, a hit. Damage is 2d6 + 3.

Critical hits

A natural 20 on an attack roll is a critical hit: it always hits, and all damage dice are rolled twice (2d6 becomes 4d6; the flat modifier is not doubled). A natural 1 always misses. Some features widen the range: the Champion Fighter crits on 19–20, for instance.

Saving throws

Many spells and traps don't use attack rolls. Instead the target makes a saving throw: d20 + the relevant ability modifier (+ proficiency if their class is proficient in that save) against a DC set by the attacker.

  • DEX save: dodge a Fireball or a falling rock. Success often means half damage.
  • CON save: resist poison, or keep concentration.
  • WIS save: resist being charmed or frightened (Hold Person, Command).
  • STR, INT, CHA saves: rarer, for being pushed, mind-blasted, or banished.

Rule of thumb: if the tooltip shows a hit chance, it's an attack roll. If it names an ability ("WIS save"), the target is doing the rolling.

Lesson 7

Spellcasting

Spellcasters have a limited supply of magic per day, measured in spell slots. Choosing when to spend them is the main strategic decision for casters.

Spell slots

Spells have a level from 1 to 6 in BG3 (the tabletop goes to 9). To cast a level 1 spell you spend a level 1 slot. Slots come back after a long rest (Warlocks get theirs back on a short rest).

You can put a spell in a higher slot than it needs, called upcasting, to make it stronger. Magic Missile in a level 2 slot fires four darts instead of three.

Cantrips vs leveled spells

Cantrips

Level 0 spells. Free, unlimited, cast any turn. They get stronger at character levels 5 and 10. Examples: Fire Bolt, Eldritch Blast, Guidance.

Leveled spells

Cost a spell slot. Much stronger effects: area damage, healing, control, summons. Examples: Healing Word (1), Misty Step (2), Fireball (3).

Concentration

Some spells last several turns and need concentration (marked on the tooltip). You can only concentrate on one spell at a time; casting a second one ends the first.

Each time you take damage while concentrating, you make a CON saving throw. The DC is 10 or half the damage taken, whichever is higher. Fail and the spell ends. This is why CON matters for casters, and why enemies target your concentrating Cleric first.

Spell attack vs save DC

Like weapons, spells either roll to hit or force a save. Both use your spellcasting ability (INT for Wizards, WIS for Clerics/Druids/Rangers, CHA for Bards/Paladins/Sorcerers/Warlocks):

Spell attack bonus = proficiency + casting modifier
Spell save DC = 8 + proficiency + casting modifier
Example: Gale at level 5

Gale has INT 18 (+4) and proficiency +3.

  • Fire Bolt (attack roll): d20 + 7 against the target's AC.
  • Fireball (DEX save): every creature in the blast rolls a DEX save against DC 15 (8 + 3 + 4). Failures take full damage, successes take half.
Preparing spells and rituals in BG3

Clerics, Druids, Paladins and Wizards prepare a subset of their spells. In BG3 you can change prepared spells any time you're out of combat, not only after a long rest. Ritual spells such as Speak with Animals or Longstrider can be cast outside combat without using a slot at all.

Lesson 8 · the important one

Where BG3 breaks from tabletop 5e

If you read one lesson, read this one. These changes are why tabletop habits (and some online advice) don't carry over, and why BG3 fights reward positioning and creativity.

1 Opportunity attacks and moving through a fight

An enemy gets a free opportunity attack (using its reaction) only when you leave its melee reach. Walking past, around or between enemies while staying in reach, or moving past one whose reach you never entered, doesn't provoke anything. That rule is the same as tabletop; what's different is how many cheap ways BG3 gives you to break away:

  • Disengage (action; a bonus action for Rogues and some others) means no opportunity attacks for the rest of your turn.
  • Misty Step and other teleports never provoke, because you don't move through the space.
  • Jump doesn't cancel them: jumping out of an enemy's reach still counts as leaving it.
  • An enemy that has already spent its reaction this round can't take an opportunity attack at all.

Hover an enemy while planning a move: if your path leaves its reach, the path line shows a warning icon.

2 Jump and Shove are cheap bonus actions

Jump costs a bonus action plus 3 m of movement and lets you leap gaps, reach ledges and cover more ground. Distance grows with STR. Many players effectively use it as a second movement.

Shove is a bonus action with 1.5 m range: your Athletics check against the target's passive Athletics or Acrobatics. Success pushes them a few metres based on your STR and their weight. Pushing an enemy off a ledge deals fall damage; pushing it into a chasm usually kills it outright (but you lose its loot).

In tabletop, Shove replaces one of your attacks and only moves the target 5 ft. In BG3, pushing enemies off cliffs is a core tactic.

3 High ground is a real bonus

Standing at least 2.5 m above your target gives +2 to attack rolls and extends the range of ranged attacks. Attacking from 2.5 m below gives −2. Tabletop 5e has no general height rule. Get your archers and casters on rooftops and cliffs; pull enemies off theirs.

4 Surfaces and elements react

Spells, potions, broken barrels and blood leave surfaces on the ground and clouds in the air. Creatures standing in them get conditions, and surfaces combine with elements.

Example combo

Karlach throws a water bottle at a group of hobgoblins (they're now Wet). Gale casts Witch Bolt or Lightning Bolt into the puddle: the water becomes electrified and the lightning damage is doubled against Wet targets. Next turn, Astarion shoots an explosive barrel next to anyone still standing.

5 Natural 20s and 1s on checks

In BG3 a natural 20 on an ability check (dialogue, lockpicking) is an automatic success and a natural 1 an automatic failure. That means even an impossible-looking DC 30 has a 5% chance. Tabletop 5e only applies this to attack rolls.

6 Karmic Dice

A setting in Options → Gameplay, on by default. It stops long bad-luck streaks: after several failures the game becomes more likely to give you a success on attacks, saves and dialogue checks. It also makes damage rolls cluster near the average. It helps enemies too. Turn it off if you want pure, honest dice.

7 Rests are tied to camp

Short rest

Taken anywhere, out of combat, twice per long rest (tabletop: unlimited, one hour each). Heals up to half of max HP and restores short-rest resources: Warlock slots, ki, Channel Divinity, Action Surge, weapon actions.

Long rest

Taken at camp and costs camp supplies (40 on normal difficulty, 80 on Tactician/Honour). Restores everything and resets your two short rests. Without enough supplies you can take a partial rest that restores only half. Long rests also advance companion stories and some timed quests.

8 Smaller changes worth knowing

  • Potions are bonus actions and can be thrown.
  • Anyone can Help a downed ally back to 1 HP with an action; no Medicine check needed.
  • Initiative is d4 + DEX instead of d20 + DEX, and party members on the same initiative share a turn.
  • Level cap is 12 and the highest spell level is 6.
  • HP gain per level is fixed, not rolled.
  • Attacking an enemy that can't see you (you're hidden or invisible) gives advantage, and Hide uses visible vision cones you can sneak around.
  • Melee attacks against a Prone or sleeping target have advantage; ranged attacks against a Prone target have disadvantage.
  • Most weapons come with special weapon actions (see Lesson 5).

Lesson 9

The twelve classes

Your class decides what your character does in a fight. Here is just enough to pick one. The "main ability" is where your highest score should go. Hit die is how tough the class is (bigger die, more HP per level).

Barbarian

STR

A furious front-liner. Rage gives bonus damage and halves physical damage taken, so Barbarians charge in and soak hits. No spellcasting and almost nothing to manage; a great first class.

Hit die d12 · Saves STR, CON

Bard

CHA

A versatile performer who mixes healing, crowd control and buffs with the best skill checks in the game. Bardic Inspiration adds dice to allies' rolls. Excellent party "face" for dialogue.

Hit die d8 · Saves DEX, CHA

Cleric

WIS

A divine caster and the classic healer, but also a capable fighter in medium or heavy armour. The domain you choose at level 1 changes the class a lot, from Life (healing) to Tempest (lightning).

Hit die d8 · Saves WIS, CHA

Druid

WIS

A nature caster with area control spells and summons, who can Wild Shape into animals like bears and spiders. Circle of the Moon druids fight mostly in beast form.

Hit die d8 · Saves INT, WIS

Fighter

STR / DEX

The weapons expert: proficient with everything, gets Extra Attack at level 5 and Action Surge for a whole extra action. Simple, reliable, and strong at any level. Works with melee or bows.

Hit die d10 · Saves STR, CON

Monk

DEX + WIS

An unarmed martial artist who fights without armour and makes several punches per turn. Spends ki on flurries, stuns and mobility. Fast and fragile; rewards careful positioning.

Hit die d8 · Saves STR, DEX

Paladin

STR + CHA

A holy knight in heavy armour who spends spell slots on Divine Smite for huge burst damage, plus auras and some healing. Bound by an oath: break it and you lose powers.

Hit die d10 · Saves WIS, CHA

Ranger

DEX + WIS

A hunter-scout, usually with a bow, backed by a handful of nature spells and sometimes an animal companion. Picks favoured enemies and terrain that give out-of-combat perks.

Hit die d10 · Saves STR, DEX

Rogue

DEX

A skill specialist with Sneak Attack, big extra damage once per turn when attacking with advantage or when an ally stands next to the target. Cunning Action lets them Dash, Disengage or Hide as a bonus action. The party's lockpicker and trap-disarmer.

Hit die d8 · Saves DEX, INT

Sorcerer

CHA

A born caster with fewer spells than a Wizard but Metamagic to bend them: twin a spell onto two targets, cast it quicker, extend its range. Pure damage and control.

Hit die d6 · Saves CON, CHA

Warlock

CHA

Gets magic from a patron. Only a couple of spell slots, but they return on a short rest and are always cast at max level. Eldritch Blast is the best damage cantrip in the game.

Hit die d8 · Saves WIS, CHA

Wizard

INT

The scholar with the biggest spell list, who can learn more by copying scrolls into a spellbook. Very fragile, but answers almost any problem with the right spell.

Hit die d6 · Saves INT, WIS
Multiclassing, briefly

BG3 lets you put levels into more than one class (for instance 2 Fighter / 10 Wizard). Your proficiency bonus follows total level. It's powerful but easy to get wrong; stick to one class on your first playthrough.

Lesson 10

Quick-reference glossary

Ability check
A d20 roll plus an ability modifier (and proficiency if trained) to see if you succeed at a task, against a DC.
Ability modifier
The bonus or penalty that an ability score adds to rolls: (score − 10) ÷ 2, rounded down.
Ability score
One of six numbers (STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, CHA) describing a character's raw aptitude.
Action
Your main activity on a turn, like attacking or casting a spell. One per turn.
Advantage / Disadvantage
Roll two d20s and keep the higher (advantage) or lower (disadvantage). Don't stack; one of each cancels out.
Armour Class (AC)
The number an attack roll must meet or beat to hit you.
Attack roll
d20 + ability modifier + proficiency, compared to the target's AC.
Bonus action
A second, smaller activity per turn: off-hand attack, potion, Jump, Shove, certain spells.
Camp supplies
Food items spent to take a long rest in BG3 (40 on normal difficulty).
Cantrip
A level 0 spell that costs no slot and can be cast every turn.
Concentration
Required to maintain some ongoing spells. Only one at a time; taking damage forces a CON save to keep it.
Condition
A status effect such as Prone, Blinded, Frightened, Wet or Burning, with specific rules attached.
Critical hit
A natural 20 on an attack roll. Always hits and doubles the damage dice.
Death saving throw
A d20 roll a downed character makes each turn. Three successes (10+) stabilise, three failures kill.
Difficulty Class (DC)
The target number for an ability check or saving throw.
Disengage
An action that stops your movement from provoking opportunity attacks this turn.
Downed
BG3's state for a party member at 0 HP. Can be revived with the Help action or healing.
Expertise
Doubles your proficiency bonus for a chosen skill. Rogues and Bards get it.
Extra Attack
A level 5 feature for martial classes: the Attack action makes two attacks instead of one.
Feat
A special ability chosen at levels 4, 8 and 12, or an increase to ability scores.
Finesse
Weapon property that lets you use DEX instead of STR for attack and damage (daggers, rapiers).
High ground
BG3 rule: being 2.5 m or more above a target gives +2 to attack rolls; being below gives −2.
Hit die
A die that represents a class's toughness (d6 to d12). Determines HP per level.
Initiative
The roll that decides turn order when combat starts: d4 + DEX modifier in BG3 (d20 + DEX in tabletop).
Inspiration
A point earned by acting in line with your background. Spend it to re-roll a failed check.
Karmic Dice
BG3 option (on by default) that prevents long streaks of failed rolls.
Long rest
Rest at camp that restores all HP, spell slots and resources. Costs camp supplies.
Natural 20 / natural 1
The number on the die before modifiers. In BG3, a natural 20 always succeeds and a natural 1 always fails on attacks and checks.
Opportunity attack
A free melee attack, using a reaction, against a creature that moves out of your reach.
Passive check
A check made without an active roll, often 10 + modifier. BG3 uses it for noticing hidden things.
Point buy
The character-creation system: 27 points to raise ability scores from 8, max 15 before bonuses.
Proficiency bonus
+2 to +4 depending on level, added to things your character is trained in.
Prone
Knocked down. Melee attacks against you have advantage; standing up costs half your movement.
Reaction
A response on someone else's turn to a trigger. One per round.
Ritual
A spell tag. In BG3, ritual spells cast outside combat don't use a spell slot.
Saving throw
A d20 roll by the target to resist an effect, against the caster's DC.
Short rest
A quick rest outside combat, twice per long rest in BG3. Restores some HP and short-rest resources.
Shove
Bonus action to push a creature away: Athletics vs their passive Athletics/Acrobatics.
Skill
A specific use of an ability, like Stealth (DEX) or Persuasion (CHA), that you can be proficient in.
Sneak Attack
Rogue feature: extra damage once per turn when attacking with advantage, or when an ally is next to the target.
Spell attack bonus
Proficiency + spellcasting modifier, added to spell attack rolls.
Spell save DC
8 + proficiency + spellcasting modifier. The number targets must meet on their saving throw.
Spell slot
A unit of magical energy of a given level (1–6 in BG3), spent to cast a leveled spell.
Spellcasting ability
The ability that powers a class's spells: INT, WIS or CHA.
Surface
A patch of fire, water, grease, ice, poison etc. on the ground in BG3 that applies effects and reacts to elements.
Surprised
A creature caught unaware at the start of combat. It skips its first turn.
Upcasting
Casting a spell with a higher-level slot than required to strengthen it.
Weapon action
BG3 special moves (Topple, Cleave, Lacerate…) granted by proficient weapons. Recharge on a short rest.
Wet
BG3 condition from water: takes extra cold and lightning damage, resists fire, can't be set Burning. Also breaks invisibility.
