Lesson 1

Overview: D&D rules, video-game body

Baldur's Gate 3 runs on a modified version of the Dungeons & Dragons 5th Edition (5e) ruleset. Almost every number you see in the game (a hit chance, a damage range, a "DC 15" in dialogue) comes from those rules.

In tabletop D&D, a human Dungeon Master reads the rules and rolls dice. In BG3 the computer does both. That's good news: you never have to do the maths yourself. But knowing what the game is calculating lets you understand why you missed, why a spell failed, or why a character "feels weak."

The whole system in one sentence

When the outcome of something is uncertain, the game rolls a 20-sided die (d20), adds some bonuses, and compares the total to a target number. Meet or beat the target: success.

Nearly everything else in the rules is detail about which bonuses get added and what the target number is.

Then a glossary to look things up. If you're about to start playing, read 1–8 and 10–12 first; the rest makes more sense once you've seen it in the game.

What does "modified" mean here?

Larian kept the core of 5e (ability scores, proficiency, d20 rolls, spell slots, classes) but changed parts that work badly on a screen or that they wanted to be more tactical: height matters more, the environment reacts to elements, several actions got cheaper, and resting is tied to a camp. Lesson 8 covers these changes one by one, and later lessons point out others as they come up.

Lesson 2

Ability scores

Every character has six ability scores, numbers usually between 8 and 20 that describe raw talent. You pick them at character creation and raise them at certain levels.

Scores become modifiers

The score itself is rarely used directly. What matters is the modifier it produces, the number actually added to rolls:

modifier = (score − 10) ÷ 2, rounded down
Score8–910–1112–1314–1516–1718–1920
Modifier−1+0+1+2+3+4+5

Modifier: +2

Practical takeaway: odd scores give the same modifier as the even number below them. Going from 16 to 17 changes nothing on its own; going from 17 to 18 gives +1. BG3 caps scores at 20 unless an item or potion says otherwise.

What each ability does in BG3

STR Strength

Melee weapon hit and damage (non-finesse weapons), how far you Jump, how hard you Shove, how much you can carry before being slowed. Skill: Athletics.

DEX Dexterity

Ranged and finesse weapon hit and damage, initiative, and Armour Class in light or medium armour. Skills: Acrobatics, Sleight of Hand (lockpicking, pickpocketing, disarming traps), Stealth.

CON Constitution

Extra hit points every level, and saving throws to keep concentration on a spell when hit. No skills. Useful for everyone.

INT Intelligence

Spellcasting power for Wizards (and Eldritch Knight / Arcane Trickster subclasses). Skills: Arcana, History, Investigation, Nature, Religion.

WIS Wisdom

Spellcasting power for Clerics, Druids and Rangers; Monk AC and ki abilities. Skills: Perception (spotting traps and hidden things), Insight, Medicine, Survival, Animal Handling.

CHA Charisma

Spellcasting power for Bards, Paladins, Sorcerers and Warlocks. Skills: Persuasion, Deception, Intimidation, Performance. These are the main dialogue skills.

Example: two fighters swing a greatsword

Karlach has STR 17 (+3). Gale has STR 8 (−1). With the same sword, Karlach adds +3 to her attack roll and to her damage; Gale subtracts 1 from both. Over a fight, that 4-point gap means Karlach hits roughly 20% more often and each hit does 4 more damage.

How scores are set at character creation

BG3 uses a point-buy system: every score starts at 8 and you spend 27 points to raise them, with a maximum of 15 before bonuses. You then add a +2 and a +1 to any two abilities you choose. A common starting peak is therefore 17 in your main ability. Levels 4, 8 and 12 give a feat, and one option is "Ability Improvement": +2 to spend across your scores.

Lesson 3

Proficiency

If ability scores are talent, proficiency is training. When your character is proficient in something, they add their proficiency bonus to the roll.

How big is the bonus?

It depends only on your total character level, not on class or ability scores. BG3's level cap is 12, so there are three steps:

Level1–45–89–12
Proficiency bonus+2+3+4

What it applies to

  • Attack rolls with weapons you are proficient with (your class decides which: a Wizard isn't proficient with greatswords, a Fighter is proficient with everything).
  • Spell attack rolls and your spell save DC. Casters are always proficient with their own spells.
  • Skills you picked from your class and background, such as Stealth or Persuasion.
  • Saving throws: each class is proficient in two of the six (a Wizard in INT and WIS, a Fighter in STR and CON).
Example: Astarion picks a lock

Astarion has DEX 17 (+3) and is proficient in Sleight of Hand. At level 3 his bonus is +3 (DEX) +2 (proficiency) = +5. At level 9 the same check is +3 +4 = +7, even if his DEX hasn't changed. Shadowheart, with DEX 13 (+1) and no proficiency, rolls with just +1.

Expertise: double proficiency

Rogues and Bards (and a few other features) can pick skills for Expertise, which doubles the proficiency bonus for those skills. A level 9 Rogue with Expertise in Sleight of Hand adds +8 from proficiency alone.

Wearing armour you aren't proficient with

You can put it on, but you get disadvantage on attacks and on STR/DEX checks and saves, and you can't cast spells. The game warns you in the equipment screen.

Lesson 4

The d20 system

Every uncertain action follows the same recipe: roll a d20, add modifiers, compare to a target. The target is called a Difficulty Class (DC) for checks and saves, or Armour Class (AC) for attacks.

d20 + ability modifier + proficiency (if proficient) + other bonuses ≥ DC → success

An ability check tests whether you can do something: persuade a guard, force open a door, recall lore. In BG3 you see these most in dialogue, where a big die appears on screen with the DC shown before you roll.

Example: a guard wants convincing, Persuasion DC 15. Your Bard has CHA +3 and is proficient (+2): total +5. You need to roll a 10 or higher on the die: 55% chance.

Before you roll, you can often add help: the Guidance cantrip adds 1d4, and spending Inspiration lets you re-roll a failed check.

Advantage: roll two d20s and keep the higher. Disadvantage: roll two, keep the lower. They're applied by circumstance: attacking a sleeping target, attacking while blinded, a spell that grants it, and so on.

  • They don't stack: three sources of advantage still means just two dice.
  • If you have both advantage and disadvantage, they cancel and you roll one die.
  • Advantage is worth roughly +3 to +5 on a typical roll.

Try it: roll a d20

Press a button to roll.

A passive check is a check nobody actively asks for. In tabletop it's a flat number: 10 + your modifier. It represents "taking an average roll."

In BG3 the biggest one is Perception: as you walk around, the game quietly checks each party member's Perception against hidden traps, secret levers and buried chests. When one succeeds, a small icon pops up over that character's head. Nobody in the party has good WIS? You'll walk past a lot of loot, and into a lot of traps.

Passive values are also used as the target for some contested actions, such as Shove (your Athletics against the target's passive Athletics or Acrobatics).

Natural 20s and natural 1s

A natural 20 means the die itself shows 20, before any bonuses. In BG3, a natural 20 on an ability check or an attack roll is always a success, and a natural 1 always fails, no matter the modifiers. (In the tabletop rules this only applies to attacks and, at many tables, death saves. See Lesson 8.)

Lesson 5

Combat fundamentals

Combat in BG3 is turn-based. Everyone rolls for position in the turn order, then each creature takes a turn with a small budget of things it may do.

Initiative and turn order

When a fight starts, every participant rolls initiative and the results are sorted into the portrait bar at the top of the screen. Highest goes first. In tabletop 5e that roll is d20 + DEX modifier; BG3 rolls only a d4 + DEX modifier, so DEX matters far more than luck. A creature that is Surprised (caught unaware) skips its first turn.

BG3 twist: party members who end up with the same initiative share a combined turn. You can switch between them freely and use their actions and movement in any order.

Example turn
  1. Shadowheart moves 4 m to get line of sight to the wounded Lae'zel (movement: 5 m left).
  2. She casts Healing Word on Lae'zel (bonus action).
  3. She casts Guiding Bolt at the goblin archer (action).
  4. She uses her remaining 5 m to duck behind a pillar.

Her reaction stays available for the enemy turns.

How BG3's action economy differs from tabletop
  • Drinking a potion is a bonus action (tabletop: an action). You can also throw a potion to heal an ally or smash it on a group.
  • Shove and Jump are bonus actions (tabletop: Shove replaces one of your attacks; jumping is part of movement).
  • Throw: you can pick up and throw almost anything, including crates, barrels and small enemies, as an action.
  • Dip: a bonus action to coat your weapon in a nearby fire or poison surface for bonus damage.
  • Weapon actions: special moves like Topple, Cleave or Lacerate come with weapons you're proficient in and recharge on a short rest.
  • Reactions can be set to trigger automatically, prompt you, or stay off, in the Reactions panel.
Hit points, going down, and getting back up

At 0 hit points a party member is Downed. On each of their turns they make a death saving throw (d20, 10+ succeeds). Three successes: stable. Three failures: dead. Taking damage while downed counts as a failure. Any ally can use the Help action on them to bring them back at 1 hit point, and any healing spell or potion does the same with more HP. A dead companion can be revived with a scroll of Revivify or by paying Withers in camp.

Lesson 6

Attacks, Armour Class & saving throws

There are two ways the game decides whether harmful things land: you roll to hit (attack roll vs AC), or the target rolls to avoid it (saving throw vs DC).

Attack rolls

d20 + ability modifier + proficiency ≥ target's AC → hit

The ability is STR for most melee weapons, DEX for ranged weapons, and your choice of either for finesse weapons like daggers and rapiers. On a hit, you roll damage: the weapon's dice plus the same ability modifier.

Armour Class (AC)

AC is how hard you are to hit. It depends on what you wear:

ArmourAC formulaTypical user
None10 + DEXWizard, Sorcerer (often boosted by Mage Armour)
Lightarmour value + DEXRogue, Bard, Warlock
Mediumarmour value + DEX (max +2)Cleric, Druid, Ranger
Heavyarmour value onlyFighter, Paladin
Shield+2 on topanyone proficient
Example: Lae'zel attacks a goblin

Lae'zel (level 4) swings a greatsword. STR 17 gives +3, proficiency +2: total +5. The goblin has AC 15 (leather armour and a shield). She needs a 10 or more on the d20. That's the "55%" the game shows when you hover the target.

She rolls 13: 13 + 5 = 18, a hit. Damage is 2d6 + 3.

Critical hits

A natural 20 on an attack roll is a critical hit: it always hits, and all damage dice are rolled twice (2d6 becomes 4d6; the flat modifier is not doubled). A natural 1 always misses. Some features widen the range: the Champion Fighter crits on 19–20, for instance.

Saving throws

Many spells and traps don't use attack rolls. Instead the target makes a saving throw: d20 + the relevant ability modifier (+ proficiency if their class is proficient in that save) against a DC set by the attacker.

  • DEX save: dodge a Fireball or a falling rock. Success often means half damage.
  • CON save: resist poison, or keep concentration.
  • WIS save: resist being charmed or frightened (Hold Person, Command).
  • STR, INT, CHA saves: rarer, for being pushed, mind-blasted, or banished.

Rule of thumb: if the tooltip shows a hit chance, it's an attack roll. If it names an ability ("WIS save"), the target is doing the rolling.

Lesson 7

Spellcasting

Spellcasters have a limited supply of magic per day, measured in spell slots. Choosing when to spend them is the main strategic decision for casters.

Spell slots

Spells have a level from 1 to 6 in BG3 (the tabletop goes to 9). To cast a level 1 spell you spend a level 1 slot. Slots come back after a long rest (Warlocks get theirs back on a short rest).

You can put a spell in a higher slot than it needs, called upcasting, to make it stronger. Magic Missile in a level 2 slot fires four darts instead of three.

Cantrips vs leveled spells

Cantrips

Level 0 spells. Free, unlimited, cast any turn. They get stronger at character levels 5 and 10. Examples: Fire Bolt, Eldritch Blast, Guidance.

Leveled spells

Cost a spell slot. Much stronger effects: area damage, healing, control, summons. Examples: Healing Word (1), Misty Step (2), Fireball (3).

Concentration

Some spells last several turns and need concentration (marked on the tooltip). You can only concentrate on one spell at a time; casting a second one ends the first.

Each time you take damage while concentrating, you make a CON saving throw. The DC is 10 or half the damage taken, whichever is higher. Fail and the spell ends. This is why CON matters for casters, and why enemies target your concentrating Cleric first.

Spell attack vs save DC

Like weapons, spells either roll to hit or force a save. Both use your spellcasting ability (INT for Wizards, WIS for Clerics/Druids/Rangers, CHA for Bards/Paladins/Sorcerers/Warlocks):

Spell attack bonus = proficiency + casting modifier
Spell save DC = 8 + proficiency + casting modifier
Example: Gale at level 5

Gale has INT 18 (+4) and proficiency +3.

  • Fire Bolt (attack roll): d20 + 7 against the target's AC.
  • Fireball (DEX save): every creature in the blast rolls a DEX save against DC 15 (8 + 3 + 4). Failures take full damage, successes take half.
Preparing spells and rituals in BG3

Clerics, Druids, Paladins and Wizards prepare a subset of their spells. In BG3 you can change prepared spells any time you're out of combat, not only after a long rest. Ritual spells such as Speak with Animals or Longstrider can be cast outside combat without using a slot at all.

Lesson 8 · the important one

Where BG3 breaks from tabletop 5e

If you read one lesson, read this one. These changes are why tabletop habits (and some online advice) don't carry over, and why BG3 fights reward positioning and creativity.

1 Opportunity attacks and moving through a fight

An enemy gets a free opportunity attack (using its reaction) only when you leave its melee reach. Walking past, around or between enemies while staying in reach, or moving past one whose reach you never entered, doesn't provoke anything. That rule is the same as tabletop; what's different is how many cheap ways BG3 gives you to break away:

  • Disengage (action; a bonus action for Rogues and some others) means no opportunity attacks for the rest of your turn.
  • Misty Step and other teleports never provoke, because you don't move through the space.
  • Jump doesn't cancel them: jumping out of an enemy's reach still counts as leaving it.
  • An enemy that has already spent its reaction this round can't take an opportunity attack at all.

Hover an enemy while planning a move: if your path leaves its reach, the path line shows a warning icon.

2 Jump and Shove are cheap bonus actions

Jump costs a bonus action plus 3 m of movement and lets you leap gaps, reach ledges and cover more ground. Distance grows with STR. Many players effectively use it as a second movement.

Shove is a bonus action with 1.5 m range: your Athletics check against the target's passive Athletics or Acrobatics. Success pushes them a few metres based on your STR and their weight. Pushing an enemy off a ledge deals fall damage; pushing it into a chasm usually kills it outright (but you lose its loot).

In tabletop, Shove replaces one of your attacks and only moves the target 5 ft. In BG3, pushing enemies off cliffs is a core tactic.

3 High ground is a real bonus

Standing at least 2.5 m above your target gives +2 to attack rolls and extends the range of ranged attacks. Attacking from 2.5 m below gives −2. Tabletop 5e has no general height rule. Get your archers and casters on rooftops and cliffs; pull enemies off theirs.

4 Surfaces and elements react

Spells, potions, broken barrels and blood leave surfaces on the ground and clouds in the air. Creatures standing in them get conditions, and surfaces combine with elements.

Example combo

Karlach throws a water bottle at a group of hobgoblins (they're now Wet). Gale casts Witch Bolt or Lightning Bolt into the puddle: the water becomes electrified and the lightning damage is doubled against Wet targets. Next turn, Astarion shoots an explosive barrel next to anyone still standing.

5 Natural 20s and 1s on checks

In BG3 a natural 20 on an ability check (dialogue, lockpicking) is an automatic success and a natural 1 an automatic failure. That means even an impossible-looking DC 30 has a 5% chance. Tabletop 5e only applies this to attack rolls.

6 Karmic Dice

A setting in Options → Gameplay, on by default. It stops long bad-luck streaks: after several failures the game becomes more likely to give you a success on attacks, saves and dialogue checks. It also makes damage rolls cluster near the average. It helps enemies too. Turn it off if you want pure, honest dice.

7 Rests are tied to camp

Short rest

Taken anywhere, out of combat, twice per long rest (tabletop: unlimited, one hour each). Heals up to half of max HP and restores short-rest resources: Warlock slots, ki, Channel Divinity, Action Surge, weapon actions.

Long rest

Taken at camp and costs camp supplies (40 on normal difficulty, 80 on Tactician/Honour). Restores everything and resets your two short rests. Without enough supplies you can take a partial rest that restores only half. Long rests also advance companion stories and some timed quests.

8 Smaller changes worth knowing

  • Potions are bonus actions and can be thrown.
  • Anyone can Help a downed ally back to 1 HP with an action; no Medicine check needed.
  • Initiative is d4 + DEX instead of d20 + DEX, and party members on the same initiative share a turn.
  • Level cap is 12 and the highest spell level is 6.
  • HP gain per level is fixed, not rolled.
  • Attacking an enemy that can't see you (you're hidden or invisible) gives advantage, and Hide uses visible vision cones you can sneak around.
  • Melee attacks against a Prone or sleeping target have advantage; ranged attacks against a Prone target have disadvantage.
  • Most weapons come with special weapon actions (see Lesson 5).

Lesson 9

Conditions

A condition is a status effect with fixed rules attached. They show as small icons under a character's portrait; hover one to see what it does and how long it lasts. Knowing the common ones tells you who to target and who to save.

The good ones

  • Hidden: enemies don't know where you are. Your next attack has advantage. See Lesson 19.
  • Invisible: can't be seen. Advantage on your attacks, disadvantage on attacks against you. Attacking or casting usually ends the Invisibility spell.
  • Hasted: double movement, +2 AC, and an extra (limited) action each turn. When it ends you are Lethargic and lose a turn.
  • Blessed: +1d4 to attack rolls and saving throws (from Bless).
How to get rid of conditions
  • Wait it out: most last a set number of turns, or allow a new saving throw at the end of each turn.
  • Break concentration: many are spells. Hit the caster and they may lose concentration, ending the effect for everyone.
  • Spells: Lesser Restoration removes blindness, disease, paralysis and poison. Remove Curse handles curses.
  • Items: an Antidote cures poison; drinking or being hit by water ends Burning.
  • Help action: wakes a sleeping ally and frees one who is stuck in a web or similar.

Lesson 10

Races

Your race (the game also calls it species) gives a handful of fixed traits: sight in the dark, a resistance, a few free spells or weapon proficiencies. It matters much less than your class, so pick what you like the look and story of.

Ability bonuses aren't tied to race. In BG3 every race gets +2 to one ability and +1 to another, and you choose which. There's no "wrong race" for a class.

What to look for

  • Darkvision (12 m) lets you see in darkness as if it were dim light. Superior darkvision reaches 24 m. Many dungeons are dark, so this is the most useful single trait. At least one party member should have it.
  • Speed: most races move 9 m; Wood Elves 10.5 m; Dwarves, Gnomes and Halflings 7.5 m.
  • Racial spells: some races get a cantrip, then extra spells at levels 3 and 5, each castable once per long rest.
  • Weapon and armour proficiencies: handy for casters, who then get better gear without spending a feat.

Custom character, Origin character or Dark Urge?
  • Custom: you design everything. The usual choice for a first playthrough.
  • Origin: play as one of the companions (Astarion, Shadowheart, Lae'zel and so on) with their own story. Their race is fixed; class can be changed.
  • The Dark Urge: a custom-looking character with a fixed, darker backstory. Best saved for a second run.

Lesson 11

The twelve classes

Your class decides what your character does in a fight. Here is just enough to pick one, plus the subclasses you'll choose between. The "main ability" is where your highest score should go. Hit die is how tough the class is (bigger die, more HP per level).

Barbarian

STR

A furious front-liner. Rage gives bonus damage and halves physical damage taken, so Barbarians charge in and soak hits. No spellcasting and almost nothing to manage; a great first class.

Hit die d12 · Saves STR, CON
Subclass at level 3: Berserker, Wildheart, Wild Magic, Giant

Bard

CHA

A versatile performer who mixes healing, crowd control and buffs with the best skill checks in the game. Bardic Inspiration adds dice to allies' rolls. Excellent party "face" for dialogue.

Hit die d8 · Saves DEX, CHA
Subclass at level 3: Lore, Valour, Swords, Glamour

Cleric

WIS

A divine caster and the classic healer, but also a capable fighter in medium or heavy armour. The domain you choose at level 1 changes the class a lot, from Life (healing) to Tempest (lightning).

Hit die d8 · Saves WIS, CHA
Subclass at level 1: Life, Light, Knowledge, Nature, Tempest, Trickery, War, Death

Druid

WIS

A nature caster with area control spells and summons, who can Wild Shape into animals like bears and spiders. Circle of the Moon druids fight mostly in beast form.

Hit die d8 · Saves INT, WIS
Subclass at level 2: Land, Moon, Spores, Stars

Fighter

STR / DEX

The weapons expert: proficient with everything, gets Extra Attack at level 5 and Action Surge for a whole extra action. Simple, reliable, and strong at any level. Works with melee or bows.

Hit die d10 · Saves STR, CON
Subclass at level 3: Battle Master, Champion, Eldritch Knight, Arcane Archer

Monk

DEX + WIS

An unarmed martial artist who fights without armour and makes several punches per turn. Spends ki on flurries, stuns and mobility. Fast and fragile; rewards careful positioning.

Hit die d8 · Saves STR, DEX
Subclass at level 3: Open Hand, Shadow, Four Elements, Drunken Master

Paladin

STR + CHA

A holy knight in heavy armour who spends spell slots on Divine Smite for huge burst damage, plus auras and some healing. Bound by an oath: break it and you lose powers.

Hit die d10 · Saves WIS, CHA
Subclass at level 1: Devotion, Ancients, Vengeance, Crown (plus Oathbreaker)

Ranger

DEX + WIS

A hunter-scout, usually with a bow, backed by a handful of nature spells and sometimes an animal companion. Picks favoured enemies and terrain that give out-of-combat perks.

Hit die d10 · Saves STR, DEX
Subclass at level 3: Hunter, Beast Master, Gloom Stalker, Swarmkeeper

Rogue

DEX

A skill specialist with Sneak Attack, big extra damage once per turn when attacking with advantage or when an ally stands next to the target. Cunning Action lets them Dash, Disengage or Hide as a bonus action. The party's lockpicker and trap-disarmer.

Hit die d8 · Saves DEX, INT
Subclass at level 3: Thief, Assassin, Arcane Trickster, Swashbuckler

Sorcerer

CHA

A born caster with fewer spells than a Wizard but Metamagic to bend them: twin a spell onto two targets, cast it quicker, extend its range. Pure damage and control.

Hit die d6 · Saves CON, CHA
Subclass at level 1: Draconic, Wild Magic, Storm, Shadow

Warlock

CHA

Gets magic from a patron. Only a couple of spell slots, but they return on a short rest and are always cast at max level. Eldritch Blast is the best damage cantrip in the game.

Hit die d8 · Saves WIS, CHA
Subclass at level 1: Fiend, Archfey, Great Old One, Hexblade

Wizard

INT

The scholar with the biggest spell list, who can learn more by copying scrolls into a spellbook. Very fragile, but answers almost any problem with the right spell.

Hit die d6 · Saves INT, WIS
Subclass at level 2: eight schools (Evocation, Abjuration, Necromancy…) and Bladesinging
Multiclassing, briefly

BG3 lets you put levels into more than one class (for instance 2 Fighter / 10 Wizard). Lesson 13 explains how it works; on a first playthrough, sticking to one class is the safe choice.

Lesson 12

Backgrounds, skills & Inspiration

Your background is who you were before the adventure. In game terms it gives you two skill proficiencies, some special dialogue lines, and a way to earn Inspiration.

The eighteen skills

Each skill belongs to one ability. You're proficient in the skills from your background plus a few chosen from your class list.

AbilitySkills and when BG3 uses them
STRAthletics: Shove, resisting Shoves, forcing things, some climbing and grappling checks.
DEXAcrobatics: resisting Shoves, keeping balance. Sleight of Hand: lockpicking, disarming traps, pickpocketing. Stealth: staying hidden.
INTArcana: magic objects and runes. History: recognising people and events. Investigation: examining clues and bodies. Nature: plants, beasts, terrain. Religion: gods and holy sites.
WISPerception: spotting traps, secrets, hidden enemies (the most important skill in the game). Insight: reading lies and motives. Medicine: examining the sick and dead. Survival: tracking. Animal Handling: calming beasts.
CHAPersuasion, Deception, Intimidation: the three main ways to win an argument. Performance: music and acting, mostly for flavour and Bards.

Backgrounds

AcolyteInsight, Religion
CharlatanDeception, Sleight of Hand
CriminalDeception, Stealth
EntertainerAcrobatics, Performance
Folk HeroAnimal Handling, Survival
Guild ArtisanInsight, Persuasion
Haunted OneMedicine, Intimidation
NobleHistory, Persuasion

SoldierAthletics, Intimidation

Picking one: choose skills your class list doesn't offer, or double up on your role. A CHA character who will do the talking wants Persuasion (Noble, Guild Artisan). A Rogue already gets Stealth, so Sage or Acolyte adds knowledge skills instead.

Inspiration

Each background has little goals: an Acolyte helping a priest, a Criminal getting away with a theft, a Sage finding a rare book. Doing one earns an Inspiration point. The whole party shares a pool of up to 4.

Spend one after failing a visible check (mostly dialogue) to re-roll it, keeping all your bonuses. It can't re-roll attacks, saving throws or background rolls like passive Perception. If you're at 4, any extra ones are wasted, so don't hoard them.

Lesson 13

Levelling up, feats & multiclassing

You earn experience (XP) from fights, quests and clever solutions. Each new level, up to 12, brings more hit points and new class features. You choose when to level: a glowing icon on the portrait means it's ready.

What you get along the way

LevelMilestone
1–3Subclass choice (level 1, 2 or 3 depending on class, see below). Quick levels.
4First feat.
5The big power jump: proficiency +3, Extra Attack for martial classes, level 3 spells (Fireball) for casters, stronger cantrips.
6–7Subclass features. Fighters get a bonus feat at 6.
8Second feat.
9–11Proficiency +4, level 5 and 6 spells, a third attack for Fighters at 11. Rogues get a bonus feat at 10; cantrips improve again at 10.
12Third feat. Level cap.

Feats

At each feat level you pick either Ability Improvement (+2 to one score or +1 to two, max 20) or a feat: a special ability. Popular choices:

Great Weapon MasterTrade −5 to hit for +10 damage with two-handed weapons; bonus attack after a crit or kill.
SharpshooterThe same −5/+10 trade for ranged weapons, and no penalty from low ground.
Alert+5 initiative and you can't be Surprised.
War CasterAdvantage on concentration saves. Great for Clerics and Druids.
Tough+2 max HP per level.
LuckyThree points per long rest to gain advantage or make an enemy re-roll.
Resilient+1 to an ability and proficiency in its saving throw (CON is the classic choice).
Polearm MasterBonus-action butt-end strike, and attack enemies as they walk into reach.
SentinelPunish enemies who attack your allies; opportunity attacks stop their movement.
Dual WielderWield two non-light weapons, +1 AC while doing so.

Rule of thumb: if your main ability is odd (17), take a feat that also gives +1, or Ability Improvement to reach 18 or 20. Otherwise damage feats for fighters and War Caster or Resilient for casters.

Subclasses

Every class splits into several subclasses that change its style. Clerics, Paladins, Sorcerers and Warlocks choose at level 1; Druids and Wizards at 2; everyone else at 3. Lesson 11 lists them per class.

Multiclassing

When you level up you may take the new level in a different class. A Fighter 2 / Wizard 10 gets Action Surge and heavy weapon skills on top of wizard magic. BG3 has no ability score requirements for this (tabletop does), but:

  • Every level outside your main class delays its big features (Extra Attack, level 3 spells).
  • Spell slots from different caster classes combine, but you learn spells separately per class.
  • Warlock slots stay separate and still come back on a short rest.
  • It's disabled on Explorer difficulty.

Recommendation: stay single-class on your first run. It's always strong enough.

Changing your mind: respec

Talk to Withers in camp (you meet him early in Act 1). For 100 gold he resets a character to level 1 and lets you rebuild them: new class, abilities, even a new starting ability spread. Levels are then re-taken instantly. Race and background can't be changed.

Lesson 14

Weapons & damage types

Weapons are sorted into simple (clubs, daggers, spears, light crossbows) and martial (swords, axes, longbows). Casters are usually trained with simple weapons only; fighters with both. Beyond that, a weapon's properties decide how you use it.

Weapon properties

PropertyWhat it means
FinesseUses the higher of STR or DEX. Daggers, rapiers, scimitars, shortswords.
LightCan be dual-wielded: attack with the off-hand as a bonus action.
Heavy / Two-handedNeeds both hands, big damage dice. Greatswords, greataxes, mauls.
VersatileOne hand (with a shield) or two hands for a bigger die. Longswords, spears.
ReachHits enemies 2.5 m away instead of 1.5 m. Glaives, halberds, pikes.
ThrownCan be thrown as a ranged attack. Daggers, handaxes, javelins.
+1, +2, +3Magic enchantment: adds that number to attack and damage rolls.

BG3 convenience: each character has a melee set and a ranged set equipped at the same time. Using whichever you need costs nothing extra. Always give your melee fighters a bow for enemies out of reach.

Damage types

Every hit has a type. That matters because creatures can be resistant (take half), vulnerable (take double) or immune (take none).

PhysicalSlashing, piercing, bludgeoning. From weapons. Skeletons are vulnerable to bludgeoning; many monsters resist non-magical physical damage.
ElementalFire, cold, lightning, acid, thunder. From spells and surfaces. Devils resist fire; Wet targets take extra cold and lightning.
MagicalForce (almost nothing resists it), radiant (great against undead), necrotic, poison (very commonly resisted), psychic.
Fighting styles and dual-wielding

Fighters, Paladins and Rangers pick a fighting style: Archery (+2 to hit with ranged weapons), Defence (+1 AC in armour), Duelling (+2 damage with a one-handed weapon and no second weapon), Great Weapon Fighting (re-roll 1s and 2s on damage), Protection, or Two-Weapon Fighting. Normally your off-hand attack doesn't add your ability modifier to damage; Two-Weapon Fighting fixes that.

Weapon actions

Being proficient with a weapon unlocks its special moves in the hotbar: Topple (knock prone) with quarterstaffs, Cleave (hit several enemies) with greataxes, Lacerate (bleeding) with slashing weapons, Piercing Shot with bows, and more. They're free extras that recharge on a short rest, so use them.

Lesson 15

Armour, equipment & magic items

Lesson 6 covered how armour sets your AC. This lesson is about the gear itself: what goes where, how to read rarity, and why magic items are the real power curve in BG3.

Armour at a glance

TypeExamples (base AC)Notes
LightPadded 11, Leather 11, Studded leather 12Full DEX bonus. Best for high-DEX characters.
MediumHide 12, Chain shirt 13, Scale 14, Breastplate 14, Half plate 15DEX bonus up to +2. Scale and half plate impose disadvantage on Stealth.
HeavyRing mail 14, Chain mail 16, Splint 17, Plate 18No DEX bonus, disadvantage on Stealth. Best for STR characters.

Equipment slots

Head, cloak, body armour, gloves, boots, amulet, two rings, main hand and off hand (melee), and a ranged weapon set. There are also cosmetic camp clothes and a musical instrument slot that have no combat effect.

Rarity

Item names are coloured by rarity: Common Uncommon Rare Very rare Legendary. Higher rarity usually means stronger or unique effects, but read the tooltip: a well-chosen uncommon ring can beat a rare one that doesn't suit the character.

BG3 vs tabletop: there's no attunement limit. In tabletop you can only use three magic items at once; in BG3 every slot can hold one. Builds often revolve around combining items, like a set of gear that adds lightning damage and another that triggers on it.

Things to check in a tooltip
  • Is it a weapon or armour type this character is proficient with? (A warning appears if not.)
  • Passive effects: "+1 to saving throws", "resistance to fire".
  • Granted spells or actions, often usable once per short or long rest.
  • Conditions it applies: many items trigger on Wet, Burning or Radiating targets.

Lesson 16

Potions, scrolls & alchemy

Consumables are single-use items. The game hands you many, and new players tend to hoard them. Use them: most fights get easier and the supply keeps coming.

Potions

Instant effects. Healing potions come in four sizes (from 2d4+2 up to 10d4+20 HP). Drinking one is a bonus action; you can also throw it to heal allies in a small area. Other potions give resistances, invisibility, flight or speed.

Elixirs

Last until your next long rest, but only one can be active at a time. Examples: Hill Giant Strength (STR 21), Bloodlust (temporary HP on kills), Viciousness (crit on 19). Drink one before a hard day.

Scrolls

A one-shot spell. In BG3 any character can read a scroll, even a Barbarian. Wizards can instead copy a scroll into their spellbook for gold to learn it permanently. Keep a Revivify scroll handy.

Throwables & arrows

Grenades (Alchemist's Fire, Acid Vial, smokepowder bombs) are thrown with an action. Special arrows (fire, ice, lightning) are used from your bow. Coatings go on a weapon for extra poison or other damage for a few turns.

Alchemy

Out of combat, open the Alchemy panel (H on PC). The process has two steps:

  1. Extract: turn three identical ingredients (herbs, mushrooms, monster parts) into one extract, like a Salt of Mugwort. This also cuts weight.
  2. Brew: combine a specific extract with a generic one (any "salt", "essence", "ash" etc.) to make potions, elixirs, grenades or coatings.

Pick up every plant you see; ingredients go into a weightless alchemy pouch. No skill check is needed.

Lesson 17

Inventory, weight & trading

Everything has weight, and characters can only carry so much. Managing that, and turning loot into gold, is a small but constant part of the game.

Carrying capacity

capacity = 40 + 10 × STR (kg)

Above 80% of capacity you are Encumbered (slower, can jump less). Close to full you are Heavily Encumbered: you can barely move and can't jump. A STR 8 Wizard has a capacity of 120 kg and becomes Encumbered from about 96 kg; a STR 18 Barbarian has 220 kg and is fine up to about 176 kg.

Keeping it manageable

  • Send to Camp: right-click any item to move it into the traveller's chest at camp. Anyone can access that chest from camp.
  • Food is heavy. Send camp supplies to camp as you find them; they're only used there.
  • Containers like backpacks and pouches can hold items; mark stuff to sell with the Wares tag so the trade screen offers it first.
  • Heavy armour, weapons you won't use and ornaments sell well; drop or sell junk rather than carrying it around.

Trading

Traders buy and sell at prices that depend on their attitude towards the character doing the trade. Attitude rises with good dialogue outcomes and with gifts of gold, and falls if you are rude or caught stealing. Let the character with the highest CHA do the shopping. Most traders also restock after each long rest.

Lesson 18

Dialogue & skill checks

Half of BG3 happens in conversations, and many of them can end a fight before it starts, or start one. Dialogue uses the same d20 check from Lesson 4, just shown on screen.

Reading a dialogue option

Options with a tag in brackets need a roll or a trait:

  • [Persuasion], [Deception], [Intimidation], [Insight], [Arcana]… a skill check.
  • [Paladin], [Drow], [Noble], [Sage]… an option only for that class, race or background, usually without a roll.

When you pick a check, the dice screen shows the DC, your bonus, and any advantage. You can then add a bonus before rolling (for example from a Bard or the Guidance cantrip) and, if it fails, spend Inspiration to re-roll.

Who is talking matters

The check uses the stats of the character who started the conversation. Before walking up to anyone important, select your best talker (high CHA, proficient in Persuasion). Other party members can join with Listen in, but don't roll.

Buff before talking

Cast Guidance (+1d4 to a check), Enhance Ability (advantage on a chosen ability's checks) or Friends (advantage on CHA checks) before starting the conversation.

Failing isn't game over

A failed check usually opens a different path, not a dead end. Many players reload to retry, which is allowed except in Honour mode; you'll often find the failure more interesting.

Hidden checks during conversations

Some checks happen automatically, without a dice screen: a passive Insight check to notice someone lying, or Perception during a scene. These can't be re-rolled with Inspiration.

Lesson 19

Stealth, light & vision

Being unseen is one of the strongest positions in BG3. It lets you set up the start of a fight, steal, or skip one entirely.

How hiding works

  1. Toggle Sneak (or use the Hide action in combat).
  2. Enemies now show vision cones. Stay out of them and you stay Hidden.
  3. Walking into a cone starts a Stealth check against their Perception. Standing in plain sight right in front of them fails automatically.
  4. Attacking from Hidden gives advantage and ends your hiding (unless you have a feature that says otherwise).

Light and obscurity

AreaEffect
Clear (well lit)Anyone in line of sight is seen. Hiding here fails.
Lightly obscured (dim light, shadow)Stealth vs Perception decides whether you're noticed.
Heavily obscured (darkness, thick fog)Can't be seen by normal sight. Those inside are effectively blinded.

A small eye icon on the character portrait shows how obscured they are. Darkvision reduces obscurity by one step for the viewer, which is why cave-dwelling enemies spot you in the dark and why you want it in your own party.

Starting fights on your terms

If your party is hidden when a fight starts, enemies that didn't see you are Surprised and skip their first turn. Common opening: sneak everyone into position, ranged characters on high ground, then open with your strongest area spell.

Invisibility vs hiding

Invisibility (spell or potion) means you can't be seen at all, even in the open, and you get advantage on your first attack. But enemies may still notice noise, and attacking or casting ends it. Hiding depends on cones and light; invisibility doesn't.

Lesson 20

Locks, traps, secrets & crime

Exploration is full of locked chests, trapped floors and hidden switches. Most of it runs on DEX and WIS checks, and on keeping the right tools in your pack.

Locks

Pick with Thieves' Tools (consumed on a failed attempt): a Sleight of Hand check. No tools? Cast Knock, find the key, or just attack the lock or chest until it breaks (noisy, and a crime if it's someone's property).

Traps

Found by passive Perception as you walk near them; a red outline appears. Disarm with a Trap Disarm Toolkit (Sleight of Hand), jump over, walk around, or trigger it from a distance with a thrown object.

Hidden things

Levers, buried chests and secret doors are also found by passive Perception. Characters with high WIS spot the most. Buried chests need a shovel.

Knowledge checks

Books, runes, bodies and odd objects often offer an INT check: Arcana, History, Investigation, Religion or Nature. Success gives lore, a hint, or occasionally loot.

Crime

Items with red names or outlines belong to someone. Taking them is stealing; entering places with a red cursor is trespassing. If an NPC sees it, they turn hostile or call the guards, who ask you to pay a fine, talk your way out (Persuasion, Deception, Intimidation), or fight.

  • Pickpocketing: while Sneaking, use Pickpocket on an NPC and make Sleight of Hand checks per item.
  • Do crimes out of sight: check that nobody's vision cone covers you.
  • Criminal, Charlatan and Urchin backgrounds can earn Inspiration from getting away with it.

Lesson 21

Party, companions & camp

You travel with up to four active party members; the rest wait at camp. Each companion has a personality, a personal quest and opinions about what you do.

Approval

Every companion keeps an approval score for your character, from −50 to 100. It changes when they witness your choices: a small "Shadowheart approves" or "Lae'zel disapproves" note appears. Only companions close enough to see or hear the event react.

  • High approval unlocks personal conversations, quest steps and romance.
  • Very low approval gets warnings; at −50 a companion leaves for good.
  • Check the current value on the companion's character sheet.

Tip: companions have opposite values. Bringing the ones who will like a given choice to that scene (and leaving the others in camp) is a normal tactic.

Camp

Camp is the hub between adventures. There you long rest (Lesson 8), talk to companions (most story scenes happen here after a rest), manage the shared chest, and meet a few special characters.

  • Withers: respec a character (100 gold), revive dead companions (200 gold), and hire hirelings.
  • Hirelings: blank-slate characters of any class you choose, without story or approval. Useful to fill a class your party lacks.
  • Swapping companions: go to camp, or use the travel menu, to change who's in the active party.
If someone dies

Party members who fail their death saves (or take huge damage while Downed) die and leave behind a dead body you can loot. Revive them with a Revivify scroll or spell (the body must be reachable), or bring them back later through Withers in camp. Death is almost never permanent outside Honour mode.

Lesson 22

Tadpoles & Illithid powers

A BG3 invention, with no tabletop equivalent. Your party carries a mind flayer parasite (a "tadpole"). Early in the game you'll find the chance to use its power, which opens a separate skill tree.

  • Each Mind Flayer Parasite Specimen you find and consume unlocks one power in the tree.
  • Powers range from utility (charm a creature, favourable dialogue) to combat abilities that cost actions or bonus actions like any other.
  • Some companions use them eagerly, some need persuading, some refuse entirely.
  • Using them is a role-play choice: some companions have opinions, and taking a lot of them has story consequences (no spoilers here).

You can skip the system entirely and still finish the game. It mostly gives flexibility and a small power boost.

Lesson 23

Difficulty modes

You choose difficulty when starting a new game. It changes numbers, not rules, and can be changed later from the options (except Honour).

ModeWhat changesFor whom
ExplorerWeaker enemies, a flat bonus to your rolls, cheaper traders, multiclassing off.You mainly want the story.
BalancedThe standard game. Camp rest costs 40 supplies.First playthrough. Recommended.
TacticianTougher, smarter enemies with +2 to hit and to their spell DCs; long rests cost 80 supplies; higher prices.You know the rules and want a challenge.

Lesson 24

Quick-reference glossary

Type to filter. Matches in both the term and its definition are shown.

Ability check
A d20 roll plus an ability modifier (and proficiency if trained) to see if you succeed at a task, against a DC.
Ability modifier
The bonus or penalty that an ability score adds to rolls: (score − 10) ÷ 2, rounded down.
Ability score
One of six numbers (STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, CHA) describing a character's raw aptitude.
Action
Your main activity on a turn, like attacking or casting a spell. One per turn.
Advantage / Disadvantage
Roll two d20s and keep the higher (advantage) or lower (disadvantage). Don't stack; one of each cancels out.
Approval
A companion's opinion of your character (−50 to 100). Changes with choices they witness; at −50 they leave.
Armour Class (AC)
The number an attack roll must meet or beat to hit you.
Attack roll
d20 + ability modifier + proficiency, compared to the target's AC.
Attitude
How a trader or NPC feels about a specific character. Affects prices; raised by gifts and good dialogue.
Background
Your character's pre-adventure life. Gives two skill proficiencies and Inspiration goals.
Bonus action
A second, smaller activity per turn: off-hand attack, potion, Jump, Shove, certain spells.
Camp supplies
Food items spent to take a long rest in BG3 (40 on normal difficulty).
Cantrip
A level 0 spell that costs no slot and can be cast every turn.
Concentration
Required to maintain some ongoing spells. Only one at a time; taking damage forces a CON save to keep it.
Condition
A status effect such as Prone, Blinded, Frightened, Wet or Burning, with specific rules attached.
Critical hit
A natural 20 on an attack roll. Always hits and doubles the damage dice.
Darkvision
Seeing in darkness as if it were dim light, usually to 12 m (24 m for superior darkvision).
Death saving throw
A d20 roll a downed character makes each turn. Three successes (10+) stabilise, three failures kill.
Difficulty Class (DC)
The target number for an ability check or saving throw.
Disengage
An action that stops your movement from provoking opportunity attacks this turn.
Downed
BG3's state for a party member at 0 HP. Can be revived with the Help action or healing.
Elixir
A drink whose effect lasts until your next long rest. Only one can be active at a time.
Enchantment
A +1, +2 or +3 on a weapon or armour, added to attack and damage rolls or to AC.
Encumbered
Carrying more than 80% of your capacity (40 + 10 × STR kg). Slows you down.
Expertise
Doubles your proficiency bonus for a chosen skill. Rogues and Bards get it.
Extra Attack
A level 5 feature for martial classes: the Attack action makes two attacks instead of one.
Feat
A special ability chosen at levels 4, 8 and 12, or an increase to ability scores.
Fighting style
A combat specialisation for Fighters, Paladins and Rangers, such as Archery or Defence.
Finesse
Weapon property that lets you use DEX instead of STR for attack and damage (daggers, rapiers).
Hidden
Not detected by enemies. Your next attack has advantage; enemies can't target you directly.
High ground
BG3 rule: being 2.5 m or more above a target gives +2 to attack rolls; being below gives −2.
Hireling
A recruitable party member without story, bought from Withers in camp.
Hit die
A die that represents a class's toughness (d6 to d12). Determines HP per level.
Honour mode
The hardest difficulty: one save file, tougher bosses, and a party wipe ends the run.
Illithid powers
BG3 abilities unlocked by consuming mind flayer parasites.
Initiative
The roll that decides turn order when combat starts: d4 + DEX modifier in BG3 (d20 + DEX in tabletop).
Inspiration
A point earned by acting in line with your background. Spend it to re-roll a failed check.
Karmic Dice
BG3 option (on by default) that prevents long streaks of failed rolls.
Long rest
Rest at camp that restores all HP, spell slots and resources. Costs camp supplies.
Natural 20 / natural 1
The number on the die before modifiers. In BG3, a natural 20 always succeeds and a natural 1 always fails on attacks and checks.
Obscurity
How visible a spot is: clear, lightly obscured (dim) or heavily obscured (dark). Affects hiding.
Opportunity attack
A free melee attack, using a reaction, against a creature that moves out of your reach.
Passive check
A check made without an active roll, often 10 + modifier. BG3 uses it for noticing hidden things.
Point buy
The character-creation system: 27 points to raise ability scores from 8, max 15 before bonuses.
Proficiency bonus
+2 to +4 depending on level, added to things your character is trained in.
Prone
Knocked down. Melee attacks against you have advantage; standing up costs half your movement.
Race
Your character's species. Gives traits like darkvision or resistances; ability bonuses are chosen freely.
Rarity
Item quality tier: Common, Uncommon, Rare, Very Rare, Legendary.
Reaction
A response on someone else's turn to a trigger. One per round.
Resistance / Vulnerability
Taking half (resistance) or double (vulnerability) damage of a given type. Immunity means none.
Respec
Rebuilding a character from level 1, through Withers for 100 gold.
Ritual
A spell tag. In BG3, ritual spells cast outside combat don't use a spell slot.
Saving throw
A d20 roll by the target to resist an effect, against the caster's DC.
Scroll
A single-use spell that any character can read in BG3. Wizards can copy it to learn the spell.
Short rest
A quick rest outside combat, twice per long rest in BG3. Restores some HP and short-rest resources.
Shove
Bonus action to push a creature away: Athletics vs their passive Athletics/Acrobatics.
Skill
A specific use of an ability, like Stealth (DEX) or Persuasion (CHA), that you can be proficient in.
Sneak Attack
Rogue feature: extra damage once per turn when attacking with advantage, or when an ally is next to the target.
Spell attack bonus
Proficiency + spellcasting modifier, added to spell attack rolls.
Spell save DC
8 + proficiency + spellcasting modifier. The number targets must meet on their saving throw.
Spell slot
A unit of magical energy of a given level (1–6 in BG3), spent to cast a leveled spell.
Spellcasting ability
The ability that powers a class's spells: INT, WIS or CHA.
Subclass
A specialisation within a class, chosen at level 1, 2 or 3 depending on the class.
Surface
A patch of fire, water, grease, ice, poison etc. on the ground in BG3 that applies effects and reacts to elements.
Surprised
A creature caught unaware at the start of combat. It skips its first turn.
Thieves' Tools
Consumable kit needed to pick locks with a Sleight of Hand check.
Threatened
BG3 condition when an enemy is next to you: ranged attacks and spells have disadvantage.
Upcasting
Casting a spell with a higher-level slot than required to strengthen it.
Weapon action
BG3 special moves (Topple, Cleave, Lacerate…) granted by proficient weapons. Recharge on a short rest.
Weapon sets
Each character has a melee and a ranged weapon equipped at once, usable without swapping.
Wet
BG3 condition from water: takes extra cold and lightning damage, resists fire, can't be set Burning. Also breaks invisibility.
